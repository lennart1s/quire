How it works

Print, fold, cut and sew your own notebook, step by step. Open Quire

What you need

  • A4 paper for the pages
  • Heavier A4 paper for the cover, as heavy as your printer takes
  • A metal ruler, a sharp knife and something to cut on
  • An awl or anything else that pokes holes
  • A needle and strong thread, best waxed linen
  • A bone folder or something else hard and smooth for the folds
  • A few clips

Sheets folded and put inside each other are called a signature, or a quire, which is where the name comes from. This notebook is one signature, sewn together with its cover. No glue needed.

1Print pages and cover

In Quire, pick a size and choose Folded spreads and Print both sides. Each spread makes four pages. The Field notes size has two spreads per A4 sheet, so 6 sheets make 48 pages and 10 sheets make 80, which is about as thick as it should get.

Design the cover and print it on the heavier paper. Keep Calculate spine width and Print in the same direction as the pages ticked, so everything folds along the same grain.

Print at 100 % / Actual size, without margins, flipped on the long edge. If front and back don't line up, use Calibrate printer first.

2Fold first

Fold the sheets before cutting them. That way you can still see the dashed fold marks, and two spreads on one sheet are folded in one go.

Fold along the marks and press the fold flat with the bone folder. Fold the cover along both lines of its spine.

3Cut along the marks

Cut out the spreads and the cover with ruler and knife, from one crop mark to the opposite one. Several light passes are cleaner than one hard one.

Stop at the corners instead of cutting to the edge of the paper. The sheet then holds together and the marks stay usable until the last cut.

4Put everything together

Put all spreads inside each other, fold into fold, and lay the cover around them, inside out: the printed side faces the pages for now.

Line everything up at the fold, top and bottom, and hold it with clips.

5Pierce the holes

Open the notebook in the middle and pierce the holes through the fold, through all pages and the cover: one exactly in the middle, then the same number towards each end, evenly spaced. Push from the inside out, onto a piece of cardboard.

Why inside out? The awl tears the paper a little where it comes out. With the cover on backwards, that side ends up inside the notebook and the outside of the cover stays clean.

6Flip the cover and sew

Turn the cover the right way around, keeping the same end up so that the holes line up. Cut a thread about three times as long as the notebook is high.

  1. Start at the middle hole, inside: push the needle out and leave a tail of about 4 cm inside.
  2. To one end and back: sew in and out through every hole up to the last one. On the way back you fill the gaps and come out at the middle again.
  3. To the other end and back: the same on the other side. You end up at the middle hole, on the outside.
  4. Finish: pull the thread tight and tie a knot around one of the two outside stitches next to the hole (a or e in the drawing). Pass the thread through to the inside, pull until the knot slips inside too and cut off the ends.

Where thread is already in a hole, push the needle past it, not through it. Pull along the fold, so the paper doesn't tear.

The drawing shows the fold from the side with five holes as an example. The letters a to h are the order of the stitches.

7Finish

Press the fold once more with the bone folder. The notebook won't close completely flat at first, that gets better with use.

In thick notebooks the inner pages stick out a little at the front edge. If you like it clean, trim them with ruler and knife, again with many light passes.
